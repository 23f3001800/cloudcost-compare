AWS S3 vs Cloudflare R2: the egress math

Both store objects behind an S3-compatible API. The difference is one line on the invoice, and at scale that line is larger than everything else combined.

The short version

S3 charges $0.023 per GB-month to store and $0.09 per GB to serve. R2 charges $0.015 per GB-month to store and $0 to serve. Storage is 35% cheaper on R2; egress is infinitely cheaper. So the answer to "which is cheaper" is entirely determined by how much data leaves the bucket.

Where S3 is still the right answer

The egress table makes R2 look unconditionally better. It is not, and pretending otherwise would get you into trouble:

  • You are deep in the AWS ecosystem. If Lambda, Athena, Glue, EMR or SageMaker read directly from your buckets, moving to R2 means paying egress out of R2 is free but your compute is now cross-cloud, adding latency and often more expensive AWS ingress paths elsewhere.
  • You need storage classes. S3 has Glacier, Glacier Deep Archive, Intelligent-Tiering and One Zone-IA. For genuinely cold data, Deep Archive at ~$0.00099/GB-month is far below R2's flat $0.015.
  • You need strong regional control. R2 abstracts location by design. Some compliance regimes require you to name and pin the region.
  • Your egress is small. Under roughly 100 GB/month, S3's free egress allowance means the two are close enough that migration is not worth an engineer's week.

The migration caveats nobody puts in the pricing table

  1. S3-compatible is not S3-identical. R2 does not implement every S3 API. Object versioning, some multipart edge cases, and several ACL behaviours differ. Audit your SDK calls before committing.
  2. You pay AWS to leave. Migrating 50 TB out of S3 costs roughly $4,500 in egress, once. At 10 TB/month of ongoing traffic that pays back in about five months, but it is real money up front and it surprises people.
  3. Class A operations are not free. R2 charges $4.50 per million writes. A workload doing heavy small-object writes or frequent LIST calls can run up a bill that the zero-egress headline hides.
  4. Egress is free, but Cloudflare's terms still apply. R2 is meant to serve your application, not to be a public CDN origin for arbitrary redistribution.

How to decide in two minutes

Work out your egress-to-storage ratio. Take last month's bytes served and divide by average bytes stored.

  • Ratio under 1 — you store more than you serve. Backblaze B2 at $0.006/GB probably beats both. Check the full comparison.
  • Ratio 1 to 3 — close call. B2's free egress allowance (3x stored volume) may cover you entirely.
  • Ratio above 3 — R2 wins, and the gap widens every month you grow.
